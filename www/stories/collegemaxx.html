The Collegemaxxer (originally written by hand) I remember a student in my language class, who sits right in front of me, second row to the front. During an assignment, he asked ChatGPT, in a serious-lockdown role, to turn his skeleton of a paragraph into an essay with certain citations, Ivy League-level quality, without purple prose, etc. He wrote something in his sparse planner, and threw it in his backpack, landing face-down, askew. In math class, he had his head down, doing practice problems with a tab on the formulas, while the teacher did an interactive demonstration. I never saw their eyes meet. His phone buzzed and lit up the AMOLED wallpaper with a notification for a Pomodoro timer, interrupting the white noise that was playing through the AirPods. And when the bell rang, I realized that this student happened to be the president of the Newspaper Club, and a member of the Debate Club leadership. I can't really remember his name, and my friends and I always just referred to him by the color of his hoodie. The only thing I really remember about him was the debate practice we had against one another, where he talked about his experience volunteering in hospitals to explain why we should make healthcare cheaper—I heard he won with cases like that at states. I don't remember his face, but I don't think I've ever seen his smile. He defeated me with a suffocating silence, creased forehead and slightly furrowed brows, and a ruthless efficiency that gave no indication on what was pushing the human behind the eyes, burrowed inside, forward.
